01 · The problem

A route announcement is a list of towns. Riders want to see the ride.

A list of towns and mileages tells riders how far, but not what the days feel like. A one-minute map shows each day on the real roads, where the rest stops fall and how much climbing is coming, so riders sign up knowing what they are in for, and your sponsors are in front of every one of them.

02 · Where it works

One route, from announcement day to the start line

  • The route reveal

    One video on announcement day shows the whole ride, town by town.

  • The sign-up page

    The route at the top of the registration page, so riders see what they are signing up for.

  • Rider emails

    A looping GIF in the countdown emails, and a clip of tomorrow's ride the night before.

  • The social countdown

    A vertical clip for each day, ready for Reels and Stories in the weeks before the ride.

  • Sponsor packages

    A place on screen is something concrete to offer a sponsor, and they can share the video too.

  • Host towns and volunteers

    Overnight towns, rest-stop crews and local press can see exactly where the ride passes.

03 · Made for rides

Yes. It’s built around the way rides run.

  • Rest stops and lunch stopsEach stop gets a card with its mile marker and what's there: water, food, mechanics.
  • Sponsors on screenOne sponsor at a time in the corner while the route plays, then all of them together on the end card. Each logo sits on a white tile, so it reads on any map.
  • The climbing, mile by mileAn elevation profile fills along the bottom as the riders go, with the total climbed so far.
  • A clip for each dayA 10 to 20 second vertical clip of each day's route, for the countdown and the morning of the ride.
  • Each day in its own colourWith a small key in the corner, so nobody mixes the days up.
  • The overnight on its own cardThe overnight town closes the day with the day's miles and what's waiting there.
  • From your route filesSend a GPX or Ride with GPS file for each day. The line follows the roads your riders will use.
  • Your event's brandYour colours, logo and type, on a map styled to match.

07 · Questions

Questions ride organisers ask

Our route isn't final yet. Can you start?

Yes. We can make the sample from last year's route and build the real one as soon as the new route is set. If a day changes after delivery, we remake the videos it affects at half their price.

Can our sponsors appear in the video?

Yes. Send us their logos. Each sponsor gets a few seconds in the corner while the route plays, and a place on the end card with the others.

Do you work from Ride with GPS or GPX files?

Yes, either. A route file for each day gives the most exact line. Without one, we follow the real roads between your stops.

How many videos does an event need?

Most rides start with one route video for the reveal. Multi-day rides usually add a short clip for each day.

What does it cost?

A route video is $179. Short clips of one day are $79 each, or $69 each when you order five or more. A seven-day ride with one route video and seven daily clips comes to $662.

How long are the videos?

Usually 60 to 90 seconds for the whole route, and 10 to 20 seconds for each day clip.

Can we keep the route under wraps until the reveal?

Yes. We send you the files and you decide when they go out. We don’t publish your video anywhere.
